## Motion principles
### Serve the experience
The purpose of animations is to clearly communicate changes in interface state or scene transitions, and to help users understand their own actions. Remove any animation that does not have a clear purpose to prevent flashy effects from overshadowing the main content and wasting system resources.
### Follow physical laws
Motion effects should follow physical laws as closely as possible and simulate real-world movement, such as acceleration, deceleration, and rebound effects. This approach not only reduces cognitive barriers but also makes animations appear smooth and believable.
### Appropriate duration and pace
Motion design should dynamically adjust the duration and easing curve based on the functional hierarchy and spatial scale of UI elements, ensuring that users can perceive interface changes without compromising the smoothness of operation.

* Small UI elements primarily for feedback or reminders should use short animation durations (< 300 ms) with fast-paced easing curves to ensure immediate and precise operational feedback.
* For large-sized elements that tend to be part of the framework or are used in specific scenarios, it is recommended to use an animation duration between 300 ms and 800 ms with a gentle easing curve to help users fully perceive spatial transitions.

### The use of the Z axis
A major difference between spatial operating systems and traditional operating systems is the introduction of Z-axis changes. Therefore, we can design Z-axis animations for UI elements. However, to avoid excessive or overly frequent changes in depth of field—which can cause users' eyes to refocus frequently and lead to eye fatigue—it is best to use only subtle and minimal animations along the Z-axis.
### Field of view edge effects
The peripheral region of the human retina is extremely sensitive to changes in light and motion (due to rod cells). Therefore, applying subtle variations in animation at the edge of the visual field is sufficient to attract user attention, while large-scale animations should be avoided to prevent discomfort.
### Implement anti-dizziness measures
In spatial operating systems, users are more sensitive to motion. When large elements that are more environmental or scene-oriented move, users may experience the illusion that they themselves are moving, which can lead to physical discomfort. This can generally be alleviated by reducing the amplitude of animations, making objects semi-transparent, among other methods.
In a spatial environment, the user perspective corresponds to the first-person camera view. Do not move the camera unnecessarily, as this may cause physical discomfort for users. If camera movement animation is required, be sure to implement measures to prevent motion sickness.
Avoid letting users rotate the virtual world. Rotation in the virtual world may disrupt the user's perception of stability, even if the rotation is controlled by the user and the movement is subtle. Alternative solution: Implement instantaneous direction change during rapid fade-out**.**
## Animation types
### **Spatial animation effects**
UI elements perform property transformation animations within a three-dimensional spatial coordinate system, covering spatial transformations such as translation, rotation, and scaling along the X, Y, and Z axes, as well as changes in properties such as opacity and color. Three-dimensional path trajectory animations are also supported. Spatial effects are primarily used to build the spatial hierarchical structure of the system and create depth perception.
### **Window animation effects**
UI elements perform property animations within a two-dimensional container, including translation along the X and Y axes, rotation, scaling, color changes, and other property transformations. The animation logic and effects are the same as those on traditional flat devices (mobile phones, tablets, and PCs).
### **Special effects**
Visual effects generated by physics engines—such as particle, fluid, cloth, and rigid body simulations—or by shader technologies, including material changes, lighting, and post-processing, are used to enhance realism and immersion. These effects typically do not directly carry interaction logic.
## Animation curve
### Elastic animation mechanism
Animation effects implemented using a spring physics model simulate the stretching and rebound motion of a spring, allowing for the realistic representation of physical phenomena such as gravity, inertia, friction, and more. These effects are well suited for animations that respond directly to user actions, for example, dragging windows or manipulating 3D models, enabling users to experience authentic and natural 'physical' interactions.
### Bezier curve animation
You can control the speed and rhythm of element movement by customizing the easing curve **cubic-bezier(x1,y1,x2,y2)**. This approach is commonly used for changes in the position, opacity, scale, rotation, and other properties of interface elements, allowing precise control over animation timing and rhythm.
